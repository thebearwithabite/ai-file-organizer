# THE LETTER

A short drama in two scenes.

---

## SCENE 1 — KITCHEN, MORNING

INT. KITCHEN — DAY

Sunlight through the window. MAYA (30s, dark hair, grey sweater) pours
coffee. On the counter sits an unopened envelope, cream paper, no return
address. She notices it mid-pour. The coffee cup stops moving.

MAYA
(under her breath)
When did this get here?

She sets the pot down, walks to the counter. Her fingers hesitate over the
envelope, then pick it up. She turns it over. The flap is sealed with wax,
pressed with a small bird.

The front door opens. TOM (her brother, late 20s, canvas jacket, a duffel
bag over one shoulder) steps in. He stops when he sees what she's holding.

TOM
Maya. Don't.

She doesn't look up. She runs a thumb under the wax seal. It cracks.

MAYA
He never sealed his letters.

Tom drops the bag. It lands soft. He takes two slow steps toward her,
hands open, palms out, like approaching a startled animal.

TOM
Whatever it says — it's just words on paper.

Maya breaks the seal and unfolds the letter. She reads. Her face does not
change, but her grip tightens until the paper creases at the corners.

CUT TO:

---

## SCENE 2 — PORCH, DUSK

EXT. PORCH — DUSK

Evening light, long shadows. Maya sits on the porch steps, the letter open
on her knees. Tom leans against the railing a few feet away, watching the
street, giving her space.

A car passes. Its headlights sweep across them and fade.

TOM
(not looking at her)
Are you going to tell me what it says?

Maya folds the letter once, twice, small enough to fit in her palm.

MAYA
He wants me to finish the house.

Tom turns. For a long moment he says nothing. Then he nods once, slow.

TOM
Then we finish the house.

Maya looks at him. She almost smiles, stops herself, and looks back at the
street. Tom sits down on the step beside her. Neither speaks. The porch
light clicks on.

FADE OUT.
